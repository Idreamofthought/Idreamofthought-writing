English Unbound
A Humanistic Adventure

After nearly thirty years in education, I am inviting adult learners and teachers to test a different kind of English learning application.

Richard M. Jenkins

01 / The idea

A different way to learn.

After nearly three decades in education, I've often wondered whether we could make language learning more human, more meaningful and more connected to everyday life.

Rather than chasing points, maintaining streaks or competing on leaderboards, learners are invited to explore a virtual world, discover new things, construct sentences and develop their confidence.

Their companion is Pip, a little apple seed with a green shoot.

Connection. Construction. Conversation.

English is the medium. Life is the subject. Curiosity is the driving force.

02 / Explore

What is inside?

  • An interactive neighbourhood and everyday English conversations.
  • Listening and pronunciation activities.
  • Personalised flashcards, sentence construction and grammar activities.
  • A discovery garden.
  • A personal workbook and learning diary.
  • Optional quizzes and guided activities.

The pilot is free, requires no account and stores learning progress locally. It currently uses scripted interactions rather than generative AI conversations.

03 / Pilot

Help the seed grow.

I am inviting adult English learners, teachers and anyone interested in alternative approaches to education to try this early pilot and share constructive feedback.

Download English Unbound — free Windows pilot (v0.4.0, 153.5 MB)

  1. Download the ZIP file (Windows 10/11, 64-bit).
  2. Right-click and select Extract All.
  3. Open the extracted folder and launch English Unbound.exe. Keep all accompanying files together.

This experimental executable is unsigned and may trigger Windows security warnings. If you are unsure, contact me before proceeding. Security review is ongoing; this is not a finished commercial release.

What works? What doesn't? What would make learning more meaningful for you? Send me your feedback.

From little seeds, great things grow.
