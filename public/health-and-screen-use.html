Screen wellbeing

A comfortable pause

Reading and playing are meant to be enjoyable. Long periods at a screen can cause temporary eye discomfort, dryness, tiredness or headaches for some people. Sitting in one position for a long time can also leave you stiff or uncomfortable.

Small steps that can help

  • Take regular breaks. Every 20 minutes, look at something about 20 feet (6 metres) away for at least 20 seconds.
  • Every hour or so, step away, move around, stretch, and get a drink of water.
  • Adjust brightness and text size to suit you, keep a comfortable viewing distance, and try to blink normally.
  • If screen use affects your sleep, leave some time away from screens before bed.

Stop if you feel unwell

If you experience eye pain, a strong headache, dizziness, nausea, unusual visual effects or other discomfort, pause and rest. People with photosensitive epilepsy may be affected by some flashing lights or patterns in digital content. Stop playing if you notice warning symptoms and seek medical advice if they continue or concern you.

These are general wellbeing suggestions, not medical advice. We make no claim that screen use causes a specific serotonin or other neurotransmitter change. If you have a health condition or concerns about symptoms, follow advice from a qualified health professional.
